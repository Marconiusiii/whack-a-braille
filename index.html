How to Play

Play a round

  • Listen for the letter, number, or braille character. Press the matching key or enter the matching braille chord before the mole disappears.
  • The game starts with QWERTY input. Choose another input method in Settings.
  • Moles get faster as the round continues. Streaks of 5 or more and fast hits earn bonus tickets.
  • After the round, keep playing or cash in your tickets for a prize. Your Prize Shelf stays with you until you clear it.

Braille input

  • Perkins Home Row uses F, D, S for dots 1, 2, 3 and J, K, L for dots 4, 5, 6.
  • Use the braille number sign before numbers.
  • With an external braille display, enter the chord and press dot 8 or Space to whack the mole.
  • Use uncontracted input for Grade 1 modes and contracted input for Grade 2 modes. In Invasion mode, enter the letter sign before a Grade 1 letter.
  • With screen-reader braille input on iPhone or Android, focus Braille Entry and type the character or contraction. Press Return if your device does not submit it automatically.

Word modes

  • Mole Battle places a short word across the board. Enter its letters in order before the word escapes. A wrong letter counts as a miss, but the word stays up.
  • Wordy Mole Mayhem places one complete word in the middle. Enter the whole word correctly before it escapes.
  • With Perkins Home Row, Wordy Mole Mayhem accepts either the uncontracted spelling or the contracted braille form.
  • Sound-alike words are spelled automatically. Turn on Spell Word to have every word spoken and then spelled.

Practice and custom games

  • Training uses 15 untimed moles without scores or tickets. Speak Braille Dots is available in Settings.
  • Mole Recon lets you practice troublesome moles or start a Grudge Match after they all escape.
  • Custom Moles lets you choose 5 targets or build an Invasion Army.

Screen readers and speech

  • With JAWS or NVDA on Windows, enter Forms or Focus mode when the game starts.
  • Choose the system voice, speech rate, and speech volume in Settings.
